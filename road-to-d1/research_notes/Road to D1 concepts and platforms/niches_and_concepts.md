# Underserved Football-Culture Niches for a Faceless Animated Comedy Series (2025-2026)

Research note, 2026-11-23. Research constraint: the session's network blocked direct page fetches for ncaa.org, si.com, espn.com, wikipedia.org and rrsn.com, so most findings come from search-result summaries of those pages rather than full reads. Figures that rest on a snippet only are marked "(snippet)". Reddit threads could not be reached directly; Reddit sentiment shows up here only secondhand.

## 1. How big are the underserved audiences?

### Takeaway
The small-college and specialist audience is large in absolute terms. About 665 NCAA football programs are projected for 2025-26, roughly 239 of them in D3, and about 94 more in the NAIA. Feeding them are about 1.03 million high-school 11-player football players, plus their parents. That pipeline is far bigger than the few thousand players who end up at the top level. One correction: the brief's "~80k+ D3 players" figure is almost certainly too high. Sourced roster numbers put D3 at roughly 22,000-27,000 players.

### Cited Findings
**High school (the feeder audience)**
- NFHS 2024-25 survey: 1,029,588 boys in 11-player football at 14,269 schools, or 1,034,425 including girls. Across all football variations (8-, 9- and 6-player, girls, etc.) the total is 1,139,326 participants. — [NFHS 2024-25 Participation Survey PDF](https://a-us.storyblok.com/f/1022696/x/85a6414d04/2024-25-nfhs-high-school-athletics-participation-survey.pdf); [NFHS story](https://nfhs.org/stories/high-school-football-continues-to-rebound-from-pre-pandemic-decline)
- 14,269 schools is the second-highest count in the survey's 55-year history, behind only 2010-11 (14,279). Boys' 11-player participation dipped by less than 1% year over year. — [NFHS story](https://nfhs.org/stories/high-school-football-continues-to-rebound-from-pre-pandemic-decline)
- Some outlets report 1,031,039 boys for the same category. That conflicts slightly with the PDF (1,029,588), so prefer the PDF figure. — [On3](https://www.on3.com/high-school/news/records-set-for-high-school-sports-participation-from-2024-25); [SI](https://www.si.com/high-school/national/high-school-sports-participation-hits-all-time-high-in-2024-25-fueled-by-growth-in-girls-programs-01k2z4rs5sgj)

**NCAA D2 / D3**
- NCAA membership page, 2025-26 projected football sponsorship: "265 | 161 | 239 | 665". The column headers did not come through, but the numbers most likely mean DI 265 (FBS+FCS), DII 161, DIII 239, total 665 (snippet; mapping inferred). — [NCAA Membership Composition and Sport Sponsorship](https://www.ncaa.org/about-us/membership-directory/membership-composition-and-sport-sponsorship/)
- D3 program counts conflict: Wikipedia lists 239 D3 football programs, while an unofficial sports site says 249 for the 2024 season. — [Wikipedia list](https://en.wikipedia.com/wiki/List_of_NCAA_Division_III_football_programs); [sportssurge (unofficial)](https://sportssurge.alibaba.com/football/how-many-d3-football-teams-are-there)
- All D3 sports in 2024-25: 210,878 championship-sport participants on 8,157 teams. All D2 sports: 141,067 participants. NCAA-wide: a record 554,298. A football-only figure by division was not in the release. — [NCAA media center, 2025-09-15](https://www.ncaa.org/news/2025/9/15/media-center-a-record-number-of-ncaa-student-athletes-participated-in-2024-25.aspx)
- D3 rosters are huge because there is no scholarship cap. Average D3 training-camp roster: about 111 (2014 article). Examples: Case Western Reserve 108, Middlebury 104. "There are no athletics scholarships, so there are no limits to the number of players on a team." — [D3football.com column (2014)](https://d3football.prestosports.com/columns/around-the-nation/2014/its-size-of-fight-in-dog); [getrecruited.college – Case Western](https://getrecruited.college/schools/case-western-reserve-university-oh/mens-football); [getrecruited.college – Middlebury](https://getrecruited.college/schools/middlebury-college-vt/mens-football)
- Football is a tuition and enrollment engine for small colleges. A D3football.com editor said driving enrollment, "especially male enrollment," is a big reason schools add football, though research finds the enrollment spikes do not last. The average D3 school with football has 2,550 full-time undergraduates (older figure). — [Texas Public Radio, 2025-09-13](https://www.tpr.org/education/2025-09-13/does-adding-football-help-colleges-with-recruiting-its-complicated); [Deadspin (older)](https://deadspin.com/how-division-iii-colleges-profit-from-football-no-one-w-1440369611/)
- D3 offers no athletic scholarships. Aid is academic or need-based only. — [NCAA (snippet)](https://www.ncaa.org/?p=7045); [Bold.org 2025](https://bold.org/blog/do-d3-schools-give-athletic-scholarships)

**NAIA / JUCO**
- NAIA: 94 football teams in 2025, 97 in 2024, 96 in 2026. — [Wikipedia 2025 NAIA season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_NAIA_football_season); [2026 NAIA season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_NAIA_football_season)
- The NAIA published a 2025 research brief arguing that bigger rosters bring in revenue but do not always keep students enrolled. — [NAIA blog, 2025-10-14](https://blog.naia.org/index.php/2025/10/14/football-roster-impacts-retention-and-revenue/)
- NJCAA: about 53 member colleges sponsored football as of November 2023 (older number). The NJCAA now runs DI and DIII football championships. — [Wikiwand/NJCAA](https://wikiwand.com/en/List_of_junior_college_football_programs_in_the_United_States); [NJCAA championship schedules](https://njcaa.org/general/2025-26/releases/20251117mel1lz)

**Specialists / long snappers**
- Kohl's runs a "National Scholarship Camp," publishes national long-snapper ratings for every class from 2017 through 2028, and says it ranks snappers for ESPN. It also claims more D1 long snappers earned full scholarships through its camps than through any other camp (company's own claim). — [Kohl's long-snapping camps](https://kohlskicking.com/camps/long-snapping-camps); [Kohl's 2028 LS ratings](https://kohlskicking.com/national-player-ratings/long-snappers/2028)
- ESPN (older, about 2015-2019) named three big national specialist camp networks: Rubio (the oldest, run by former UCLA snapper Chris Rubio), Kohl's, and Pro Snapper. It described twice-yearly national camps in Las Vegas. — [ESPN Playbook (older)](https://www.espn.com/blog/playbook/fandom/post/_/id/21533/lines-growing-long-at-long-snapping-camps)
- Real example: Morrow Evans was ranked No. 2 long snapper in the 2024 class by Rubio, got a Mississippi scholarship offer, and took a preferred walk-on spot at Ohio State. He built his recruitment partly through YouTube videos. — [Yahoo Sports](https://sports.yahoo.com/youtube-videos-helped-long-snapper-110045732.html)
- A long snapper's transfer from Washington to Florida made news. Snappers now have portal stories too. — [SI/Washington](https://www.si.com/college/washington/football/husky-snapper-snapped-up-in-transfer-portal-by-florida-01kf1q8eearn)
- A 2026 article quotes a new Maryland long snapper describing his goal as being a "ghost" at an "undervalued spot." That attitude is exactly the deadpan snapper persona. — [SI/Maryland](https://www.si.com/college/maryland/football/maryland-s-new-long-snapper-aims-to-be-ghost-at-undervalued-spot-01kgte1vfbyv)
- A local feature explains "how a long snapper can get a scholarship," which shows the position is its own recruiting genre. — [AOL/LA Times Prep talk](https://www.aol.com/news/prep-talk-jackson-reach-mira-111500224.html)

### Inferences
- About 1.03M high-school players plus parents, coaches and siblings is a multi-million audience that is touched by recruiting anxiety but sees almost no comedy made about the small-college outcome where most college players actually land.
- Using 239 D3 programs × 95-111 players gives roughly 22,700-26,500 D3 players a year. Add D2 (161 programs) and NAIA (94), and the non-D1 college football world is about 500 programs, probably 45,000-60,000 players. The "80k+ D3 players" figure in the brief is likely conflated and should not be used.
- Each D3 player brings parents, grandparents and alumni who watch games through local streams and live-score sites. That audience overlaps directly with the owner's D3 scores website.

### Gaps
- No official 2024-25 football-only participant count by NCAA division. The NCAA Sport Sponsorship and Participation dashboard has it, but ncaa.org was blocked in this session.
- No reliable count of D1 scholarship long snappers per year, and no camp prices for Rubio, Kohl's or Sailer. Chris Sailer Kicking was not surfaced by any search.
- No sourced figure for the share of HS players who play in college at all, or who play at D1. NCAA "probability of competing" data would answer it but could not be fetched.
- Offensive linemen, walk-ons and HS coaches have no audience-size data beyond the totals above.

## 2. Which real recurring absurdities are rich comedy material?

### Takeaway
The best material has documented sources: "offers" that aren't offers, a paid camp and recruiting-service economy aimed at parents, D3's no-scholarship-but-110-man-roster economics, specialist rankings maintained by the camps themselves, a transfer portal with thousands of entries in days, and sideline-parent behaviour.

### Cited Findings
- **Non-committable offers.** SI (2025-26) calls the non-committable offer "one of recruiting's open secrets." It says some programs use offers as marketing, but that the offers still "create momentum" and "validate film." — [SI High School](https://www.si.com/high-school/recruiting/the-hidden-truth-about-college-football-offers-what-recruits-don-t-know-is-hurting-them-01kb8aj5z4sj) (snippet)
- An older recruiting explainer says most early offers are "bogus." If a coach no longer wants the player, "you just forget to call him come spring." — [Bleacher Report (2009)](https://bleacherreport.com/articles/201519-college-football-recruiting-101-the-offer-game-how-it-all-works)
- A fan-site explainer: there is no contract when a scholarship is offered, so even an official offer is "largely meaningless." — [Give 'Em Hell Brigham](https://giveemhellbrigham.com/recruiting-for-dummies-chapter-2-how-much-value-do-you-want-your-offer-to-have)
- **Paid camps.** At "mega camps" players must pay to attend, and they mainly help smaller colleges that can't afford to travel to recruit. — [Sports360AZ](https://sports360az.com/zone-read-showcase-or-simply-for-show); [Stack, "Should I Go to This Camp?"](https://www.stack.com/?p=298982)
- **The recruiting-service economy aimed at parents.** National Scouting Report was ordered by the NY Attorney General to pay $20,180 in penalties and restitution for taking parents' money without delivering. A listed red flag: paying a fee to be introduced to a coach "who will offer a scholarship." — [Sports Destinations](https://www.sportsdestinations.com/management/education/sports-parents-being-targeted-fly-night-college-17518)
- NCSA felt it had to publish a page titled "Is NCSA a scam?" A review aggregator says most Reddit forum opinion calls it "not worth it," while it holds a BBB A+ rating. — [NCSA blog](https://www.ncsasports.org/blog/is-ncsa-a-scam); [DeleteMe review](https://joindeleteme.com/is-it-scam/is-ncsa-a-scam/)
- A recruiting Twitter account ("D1 Bound") was suspended after a USA Today investigation. Pay-for-exposure social accounts are a documented phenomenon. — [Awful Announcing](https://awfulannouncing.com/high-school/d1-bound-recruiting-twitter-account-suspended-usa-today-investigation.html)
- **Camps that rank the kids who pay to attend.** Kohl's ratings cover only snappers its own coaches have watched at a Kohl's camp. — [Kohl's LS ratings](https://kohlskicking.com/national-player-ratings/long-snappers/2026)
- **The portal flood.** The 2025 winter portal passed 3,310 entrants by day two. A spring 2026 window had more than 2,600 FBS scholarship players enter on day one. — [CFN](https://collegefootballnetwork.com/how-many-players-have-entered-the-transfer-portal-2025/); [Front Office Sports](https://frontofficesports.com/cfbs-record-transfer-portal-surge-hits-3200-and-counting)
- The House settlement (effective July 1, 2025) brought D1 revenue sharing and roster caps. D3 rules did not change. — [Bold.org](https://bold.org/blog/do-d3-schools-give-athletic-scholarships)
- **D3 economics.** No scholarships, so no roster limit, and "coaches may 'offer' additional roster spots." Every player pays tuition or uses regular aid, so each extra player is extra revenue. — [Deadspin (older)](https://deadspin.com/how-division-iii-colleges-profit-from-football-no-one-w-1440369611/); [D3football.com (2014)](https://d3football.prestosports.com/columns/around-the-nation/2014/its-size-of-fight-in-dog)
- **Sideline parents.** Viral clips of youth-football parent brawls (an 11U game) and sideline-etiquette speeches circulate widely. One writer compared youth-sports stage parents to "Dance Moms." — [AOL/Daily Caller](https://www.aol.com/news/wild-brawl-erupts-between-ton-183108230.html); [Scary Mommy](https://www.scarymommy.com/parenting/every-sports-parent-needs-to-hear-this-coachs-speech-about-sideline-ettiquitte); [youth.inc](https://www.youth.inc/learn/what-does-your-parent-yell-from-the-sidelines)
- School "pay-to-play" participation fees: 48% of Michigan high schools charged one in 2019-20, down from 57% in 2013-14 (older). An Idaho parent cited a $110 fee. — [MHSAA](https://mhsaa.com/topics/mhsaa-news/pay-plays-extent-unchanged-15-16?page=42); [Idaho EdNews](https://www.idahoednews.org/?p=35271)
- **The "40U" phenomenon.** Viral clips of men around 40 going all-out in organized adult tackle leagues spread across X, Instagram and TikTok. — [Complex](https://www.complex.com/sports/a/markelibert/viral-clips-of-40u-football-leagues-has-people-rolling-with-laughter)

### Inferences
- The comedy engine running through all of these is a gap between status and reality: an offer that isn't an offer, a ranking from the camp you paid, a "D1 Bound" account that isn't, a roster spot that is really a tuition bill. That gap suits a deadpan character like Gus.
- D3-specific absurdities are well known inside the culture but not documented above: 7am lifts, academic-first recruiting pitches, long bus trips, players who also run the student radio station, coaches who recruit 40 kids per position. Each episode can draw on these without citing anything. They are "known by insiders", not sourced, so treat them as writer knowledge, not facts.

### Gaps
- Reddit threads (r/CFB, r/footballstrategy, r/highschoolfootball, r/D3football) could not be fetched. No first-hand thread quotes.
- No sourced 2025-26 cost figures for a typical summer camp circuit, Hudl subscriptions, or the share of families buying recruiting services.
- No survey data on recruit or parent anxiety about NIL or offers.

## 3. Which niches have live-action creators or meme accounts but no animated series?

### Takeaway
Animated football comedy exists but sits at the top tier. Bleacher Report's Gridiron Heights covers the NFL, its new Big College Noon Day covers big-time college football, and 442oons covers soccer. No search found an animated series about high-school recruiting, D2/D3/NAIA/JUCO, specialists or football parents. Live-action demand around those niches does exist: viral "meme kid" linemen, YouTube-built long-snapper recruitments, 40U league clips, sideline-parent videos and creator-athletes.

### Cited Findings
- **Gridiron Heights** (B/R, NFL): a weekly minute-long animated series with "more than 55 million views" by a later press release, 30M+ earlier. — [WBD press](https://press.wbd.com/ca/media-release/tnt-sports/bleacher-reports-br-gridiron-kicks-all-new-content-lineup); [Digiday](https://digiday.com/media/bleacher-report-now-three-hit-animation-series)
- Another source says B/R's longest-running animated series has 10 seasons and over 150M views. This conflicts with the 55M press figure; the two likely come from different dates or count across platforms. — [Cartoon Brew](https://www.cartoonbrew.com/shorts/bleacher-report-br-cartoons-zach-mccann-261027.html)
- **Big College Noon Day** (B/R, 2026): a weekly animated college-football pregame show on YouTube. It covers big programs and game predictions, not small-college life. B/R is also testing an 8-15-minute "cartoon meets talk show meets sports vodcast" format. — [Bleacher Report](https://bleacherreport.com/articles/25495867-watch-debut-episode-brs-new-animated-series-big-college-noon-day); [Cartoon Brew](https://www.cartoonbrew.com/business/bleacher-report-br-cartoons-zach-mccann-261027.html)
- B/R's cartoon YouTube channel got over 20,000 subscribers in its first week. — [Digiday](https://digiday.com/media/bleacher-report-launches-youtube-channel-for-its-sports-cartoon-fanbase-ahead-of-world-cup/)
- **442oons** (UK soccer cartoons, since 2013) has moved to TikTok and Reels and is listed at about 8M followers. It shows a single faceless animated sports-comedy brand can scale. — [Tackle From Behind](https://tacklefrombehind.com/?p=8857); [Tubefilter](https://www.tubefilter.com/tag/442oons/)
- TikTok launched a "Footy Corner" (soccer) community hub in January 2026. The platform is investing in sport-community discovery. — [TikTok Newsroom](https://newsroom.tiktok.com/introducing-footy-corner?lang=en-GB)
- **Small-college lineman as meme.** Dieunerst Collin, the "Popeyes meme kid," became a D2 offensive lineman at Lake Erie College, had 63.6k Instagram followers (January 2023), and landed a Popeyes NIL deal. — [Bleacher Report](https://bleacherreport.com/articles/10061269-d-2-ol-dieunerst-collin-gets-nil-contract-with-popeyes-because-of-popular-meme); [SportBible](https://www.sportbible.com/nfl/viral-meme-kid-looks-unrecognisable-as-huge-college-football-player-404062-20230110)
- **Small-school player goes viral.** A Davidson (FCS, non-scholarship) player went viral with a fake NFL Draft post. — [Yahoo](https://ca.news.yahoo.com/real-story-davidson-football-player-103000674.html)
- **Athlete-creators.** Hunter Reynolds (Utah State DB) and Nana Appouh (UVA, 2025) turned football careers into creator careers. Both are FBS. — [Utah Business](https://www.utahbusiness.com/hunter-reynolds-turned-his-football-career-into-a-tiktok-career/); [Cavalier Daily, 2025-12](https://www.cavalierdaily.com/article/2025/12/from-football-player-to-content-creator-how-nana-appouh-does-it-all)
- **Long snappers on YouTube.** Morrow Evans built his recruitment with YouTube snapping videos. — [Yahoo Sports](https://sports.yahoo.com/youtube-videos-helped-long-snapper-110045732.html)
- **Kid-football influencer culture.** The "Baby Gronk" phenomenon (a 10-year-old online football celebrity whose recruiting buzz became a meme) shows a huge appetite for satire of hyped-up youth recruiting. — [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/pop-culture/baby-gronk-livvy-rizz-explainer-rcna88392); [Know Your Meme](https://knowyourmeme.com/editorials/h00pify-talks-brainrot-the-baby-gronk-rizzed-up-livvy-dunne-meme-collaborating-with-the-costco-guys-and-more)

### Inferences
- The white space is real. Animated football comedy is built around stars (NFL, big college programs). Nothing animated surfaced for the 1M-player recruiting funnel, small colleges or specialists. Big College Noon Day shows B/R is moving into college, but toward the big programs, not D3.
- Baby Gronk shows recruiting-hype satire has mass reach. Animation lets that satire be done without using a real child, which keeps it family-safe and avoids real-person issues.
- A faceless animated format avoids the main weakness of live-action creators in this niche: they are tied to one player's career and age out.

### Gaps
- No D3-specific TikTok or IG meme account with verified follower counts was found. Accounts likely exist, such as D3football.com socials and team-run accounts, but did not appear in search. Check manually on TikTok with #d3football and #d3.
- No verified follower counts for long-snapper or kicker creator accounts (e.g., camp brands' TikToks).
- No search was run for small animated creators (under 100k) making recruiting jokes. Absence in search is not proof of absence.

## 4. Proposed series concepts (6-10), with evidence for demand

All concepts use fictional schools, players and companies. Fictional stand-ins are suggested, e.g. "Hudl" becomes "TapeDrop", "NCSA" becomes "ScoutPro Elite", "Kohl's/Rubio" becomes "Snapworks National Showcase".

### Takeaway
There are eight concepts: three variants of Road to D1, and five new shows built on a specialist, a parent, a coordinator, a D3-season or a JUCO engine. The highest-value ones combine a large audience (HS players and parents) with a direct link to the D3 scores site, especially Concept 4, which runs on real weekly D3 results.

### Cited Findings (evidence behind each concept)
1. **Road to D1: Offer Season (pilot continuation).** Tre (flashy 3-star WR) chases star ratings and "blessed to receive" posts. Gus (deadpan long snapper) keeps getting recruited by accident. *Engine:* each episode is one recruiting ritual (camp, junior day, edit-photo shoot, DM, official visit, portal rumor) that Tre fails at and Gus succeeds at without trying. *Running gag:* Gus's offer count on screen vs. Tre's "offer" that was really a camp invite. *Evidence:* non-committable offers as an "open secret" ([SI](https://www.si.com/high-school/recruiting/the-hidden-truth-about-college-football-offers-what-recruits-don-t-know-is-hurting-them-01kb8aj5z4sj)); real snappers ranked and offered by camp networks ([Kohl's](https://kohlskicking.com/national-player-ratings/long-snappers/2026); [Yahoo/Evans](https://sports.yahoo.com/youtube-videos-helped-long-snapper-110045732.html)).
2. **Road to D1: Freshman Year at D3 (time-skip variant).** Tre ends up at a fictional D3 school, Hollow Creek College, as one of 14 receivers on a 115-man roster with no scholarship. Gus is at a power school, snapping for 22 seconds a season and bored. *Engine:* a split-screen contrast each episode (7am lift vs. private chef; charter bus to a game 5 hours away vs. charter jet). *Evidence:* D3's roster sizes and no-scholarship economics ([D3football.com](https://d3football.prestosports.com/columns/around-the-nation/2014/its-size-of-fight-in-dog); [Deadspin](https://deadspin.com/how-division-iii-colleges-profit-from-football-no-one-w-1440369611/)); 239 D3 programs ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.com/wiki/List_of_NCAA_Division_III_football_programs)).
3. **Road to D1: Gus's POV (spin-off micro-format).** Ten-second deadpan "snapper diaries": Gus narrates one snap, one camp ranking, or one coach mispronouncing his name. *Engine:* very cheap to make (one character, one line), easy to loop, and it feeds the main show. *Evidence:* "ghost at an undervalued spot" persona ([SI/Maryland](https://www.si.com/college/maryland/football/maryland-s-new-long-snapper-aims-to-be-ghost-at-undervalued-spot-01kgte1vfbyv)); a specialist camp industry with national rankings for every class ([Kohl's](https://kohlskicking.com/camps/long-snapping-camps)).
4. **Saturday at Hollow Creek (D3 season synced to real results).** A fictional D3 team plays a fictional schedule, but each Monday's episode reacts to a real D3 storyline: an upset score, a 70-point game, a 4OT finish. The storyline is pulled from the owner's live-scores site, with no real school names on screen. *Characters:* a coach who is also the AD and bus driver, a QB who is a chemistry major, a kicker who is the student-body president, a 41-year-old radio play-by-play guy. *Engine:* a weekly calendar beat, plus "link in bio: real D3 scores" cross-promotion. *Evidence:* about 239 D3 programs and 110-man rosters, which means many families watching ([NCAA sponsorship snippet](https://www.ncaa.org/about-us/membership-directory/membership-composition-and-sport-sponsorship/)); football as an enrollment engine ([TPR 2025](https://www.tpr.org/education/2025-09-13/does-adding-football-help-colleges-with-recruiting-its-complicated)); no animated D3 series found (Section 3).
5. **The Snap Count (long-snapper-only universe).** A world where long snapping is the only sport that matters: a fictional "Snapworks National Showcase" ranks 13-year-olds on a 5-star scale, there's a snapper draft-day show, and snapper dynasties span generations. *Engine:* mockumentary parody of big-recruit coverage, applied to the least glamorous position. *Evidence:* national long-snapper ratings for the 2017-2028 classes ([Kohl's](https://kohlskicking.com/national-player-ratings/long-snappers/2028)); camps with national scholarship showcases in Las Vegas ([ESPN](https://www.espn.com/blog/playbook/fandom/post/_/id/21533/lines-growing-long-at-long-snapping-camps)); snapper transfer-portal news ([SI](https://www.si.com/college/washington/football/husky-snapper-snapped-up-in-transfer-portal-by-florida-01kf1q8eearn)).
6. **Bleacher Row (football parents).** Four parents on the same bleacher row every Friday night: the stat-tracking dad with a clipboard, the mom who runs the team's fan account, the grandpa who thinks it's 1978, and the parent who already paid for three recruiting services. *Engine:* one game per episode, told only through the parents' reactions (no on-field action, so cheap to animate). *Evidence:* parents targeted by recruiting-service fraud ([Sports Destinations](https://www.sportsdestinations.com/management/education/sports-parents-being-targeted-fly-night-college-17518)); an "Is NCSA a scam?" page existing at all ([NCSA](https://www.ncsasports.org/blog/is-ncsa-a-scam)); viral sideline-parent content ([Scary Mommy](https://www.scarymommy.com/parenting/every-sports-parent-needs-to-hear-this-coachs-speech-about-sideline-ettiquitte); [AOL](https://www.aol.com/news/wild-brawl-erupts-between-ton-183108230.html)); a 1M-player HS base, each with parents ([NFHS](https://a-us.storyblok.com/f/1022696/x/85a6414d04/2024-25-nfhs-high-school-athletics-participation-survey.pdf)).
7. **Inbox Zero (the recruiting coordinator's inbox).** A burned-out D3 recruiting coordinator at a fictional college gets 400 highlight-tape emails a day. Each episode is one: a 9-minute tape of a kid's kickoffs, a dad's 2,000-word email, a "5-star" ranked by a service no one has heard of, a punter who sends only slow-motion clips. *Engine:* the tape is the joke. Screen-recording-style animation is cheap. *Evidence:* D3 coaches "offering" unlimited roster spots for tuition ([D3football.com](https://d3football.prestosports.com/columns/around-the-nation/2014/its-size-of-fight-in-dog)); a pay-for-exposure ecosystem ([Awful Announcing – D1 Bound](https://awfulannouncing.com/high-school/d1-bound-recruiting-twitter-account-suspended-usa-today-investigation.html)); portal volume in the thousands ([CFN](https://collegefootballnetwork.com/how-many-players-have-entered-the-transfer-portal-2025/)).
